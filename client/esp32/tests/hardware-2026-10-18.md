# ESP32 retry verification — 2026-10-18

The retry update built and flashed successfully to the connected XIAO ESP32-S3.
All three host checks passed, including the actual retry helper's 3/6/12/24/48/60
second sequence, sustained saturation at 60 seconds, reset and independent counters.

Board serial showed Wi-Fi retry waits of 3, 6, 12, 24, 48 and 60 seconds, followed
by repeated 60-second waits. Microphone capture continued at about 16 ksample/s
with zero read errors. The orange LED's disconnected patterns are scheduled by a
separate task every five seconds throughout the wait: single flash for Wi-Fi,
double flash for Apollo. No visual timing measurement was performed.

This run did not produce a Wi-Fi-ready event. Apollo connection and backoff reset
after successful reconnection remain host-tested rather than network-validated.
No Apollo changes or physical home actions were performed. Private credentials
were not inspected and remain ignored, as do the compiled firmware artifacts.

See [firmware behavior and earlier microphone checks](../README.md).

## Connection diagnosis after the retry check

A subsequent restart joined the access point and acquired an IP after about ten
seconds. Apollo's LAN ingress accepted the named client and received bounded PCM,
but sessions repeatedly disconnected. ESP transport logs showed socket write
timeouts, followed by the firmware's frontend/send failure and session cleanup.

Tested TCP_NODELAY with explicitly owned TCP/WebSocket transports. A 90-second
board observation still showed repeated send failures; this did not fix the issue.
Removed that experiment and restored the standard WebSocket transport. Apollo and
the 100 ms write deadline, audio age limit and queue bounds remain unchanged.

Added Wi-Fi IP/RSSI/channel, disconnect reason, failed-send elapsed time and byte
count, and session-interruption diagnostics. The built/flashed board measured
RSSI of -89 to -92 dBm; failed 640-byte writes took 103,768 and 104,715 microseconds
at -91 dBm while still associated. This establishes a very weak RF link alongside
the observed send-deadline failures. The maintainer then confirmed that no external
antenna was attached. Attach the supplied antenna and remeasure signal/stability
before declaring the connection fixed. Credentials were not read.

Seeed [documents external antenna installation](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/xiao_esp32s3_wifi_usage/)
and notes that Wi-Fi may fail without it. Signal/stability after a physical change,
spoken transcription, echo rejection and end-to-end latency remain unverified.

## Supplied antenna attached

The maintainer attached the external antenna. A subsequent 75-second serial
observation showed one continuously advancing stream with no send failures,
disconnects, queue/capture faults or reconnect messages. Sent-frame counters rose
from 315 to 2,631 (2,316 additional 640-byte PCM frames). Apollo returned four
accepted and four completed request statuses. This supports the missing antenna
as the cause of the previously observed transport instability.

No restart was forced during the active conversation, so this window has no fresh
Wi-Fi-ready RSSI measurement. Long-duration stability, exact spoken transcription,
echo rejection and physical playback timing still need separate checks. Request
completion is not physical playback completion.

## Earlier build provenance

The compatible 2026-10-18 build used 909,409 bytes of application flash and 53,292
bytes of static RAM (initial baseline: 896,469 / 37,148). Private settings increased
flash to 909,541 bytes. Extra static RAM is mostly bounded pre-roll; dynamic
allocations are excluded. The connected ESP32-S3 revision 0.2 has 8 MB flash and
embedded 8 MB PSRAM, matching the configured flash header. Three host checks and
90 client/shared-path regressions passed. Later LED/retry/diagnostic builds also
passed the three host checks and flashed successfully. Password contents were not
inspected; private settings and compiled firmware remain ignored by Git.
